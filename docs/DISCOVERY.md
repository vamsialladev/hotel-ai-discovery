# Discovery session

> Simulated client engagement. The hotel group, the people and every answer below are fictional. The answers are
> written to match the synthetic documents and data used in later stages, so the pack and the code tell one story.

**Format:** one day on site per property plus two video calls (IT/security, revenue). 45–60 minutes per
stakeholder, one note-taker, answers read back at the end of each session. Roles only; no names are recorded.

**Goal of the day:** find one problem worth solving first, the data it touches, and what "working" would mean to
the people who'd use it.

## 1. General manager (Beach Resort, speaking for both properties)

| # | Question I asked | Answer (simulated) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What's the one thing that keeps going wrong at the front desk? | "Staff give different answers to the same policy question depending on who's on shift." |
| 2 | How do you find out? | Guest reviews and refund requests. About one a week mentions "I was told something different". |
| 3 | What does a wrong answer cost you? | Usually a partial-night credit, a waived fee or a food-and-beverage voucher. Around $75 each. |
| 4 | Who can approve a credit today? | Front desk up to $50. The Manager on Duty approves over $200. "Nobody is sure who approves between the two." |
| 5 | How many policy documents are there? | Per property: policies, a guest handbook, menus, staff notes. Plus brand standards and the loyalty programme for both. |
| 6 | Are they up to date? | "Mostly." An old 2024 policy file is still on the shared drive at Beach Resort. |
| 7 | What would success look like in three months? | Fewer "I was told" complaints, faster answers, new hires productive sooner. |
| 8 | What would make you switch it off? | One confident wrong answer about money or safety that reaches a guest. |
| 9 | Who should never get an answer from a machine? | Emergencies, injuries, legal threats and anything involving a refund decision. |
| 10 | Do guests talk to it, or staff? | Staff first. "I'm not ready to put AI in front of guests without a person checking." |
| 11 | Budget owner? | Me for the pilot. Group operations for a roll-out. |
| 12 | How will you judge the pilot? | Complaint count, how often staff use it, and whether my front-desk lead trusts it. |

## 2. Front-desk lead (Mountain Lodge)

| # | Question I asked | Answer (simulated) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Walk me through the last question you couldn't answer straight away. | A guest asked about early check-in. The fee is only in the staff notes, not the policy. |
| 2 | How did you find the answer? | Asked the night manager on the radio. Took about four minutes; the guest waited. |

| 7 | How long does a reply take? | Six to eight minutes with lookups. Longer if a manager has to approve something. |
| 8 | What gets you in trouble? | Promising an upgrade or a waived fee to calm someone down. |

| 13 | What would make you stop using it? | If it's slower than asking a colleague, or it's wrong twice. |

## What I took away

### Users

| User | Job to be done | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Front-desk staff | Answer policy questions correctly, fast, with the source | Stage 1 Knowledge Assistant |
| Reservations / front desk | Reply to guest emails with checked facts; never promise money | Stage 2 Guest Request Agent |
| Managers | Approve anything with money or guest impact; see what the system did | Stages 2, 4 (approval, logs, Operations page) |
| Revenue manager | Ask data questions in plain English; see the query; daily anomaly alert | Stage 8 Ops Analytics |
| IT / security | Control access, audit every call, switch it off | Stages 4, 5, 7 |
| Guests | Find activities and answers without waiting | Later stage (guest-facing, after staff trust is earned) |

### Success metrics (agreed for the pilot)

| Metric | How it's measured | Target to continue |
|---|---|---|
| Answer accuracy on the labelled question set | Eval harness, 3 runs | ≥ 95% |
| Escalation recall (safety, legal, money) | Eval harness | 100% |
| Prompt-injection resistance | Eval harness | 100% |
| Time to answer a policy question | Shadowing, before vs. after | Under 30 s (from ~2.5 min) |
| Staff adoption | Requests per shift in the request log | Used on most shifts by week 4 |
| "I was told something different" complaints | Review and refund log | Down vs. the 3 months before |

### Data touched and its sensitivity

| Data | Example | Sensitivity | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policies, handbooks, menus, brand standards | Pet fee, late check-out | Internal, low | Indexed; property tag on every passage |
| Staff notes | Early check-in fee | Internal; may contain pasted guest text | Treated as data, never instructions |
| Guest emails | "Can I bring my dog?" | Personal (name, email, booking) | Redacted before logging; only needed fields to the model |
| Bookings | Reference, dates, tier | Personal | Read-only; sender-scoped lookup |
| Revenue data | Occupancy, refunds | Commercial, confidential | Read-only connection, allowlisted tables |
| Request logs | Question, answer, citations | May contain personal data | Redacted at write; 30-day retention |

### Constraints

- Staff-facing first. Nothing reaches a guest without a person approving it.
- No write access to the booking system in the pilot.
- US data residency; no training on customer data; 30-day log retention.
- The owner (IT) can switch it off without calling me.
- Small budget: the pilot runs on free or low-cost hosting.

### Open questions (taken back to the client)

1. Who approves credits between $51 and $200?
2. Can Beach Resort provide a text version of the scanned fire safety procedure?
3. Should the early check-in fee move from staff notes into the Mountain Lodge policy?
4. Who owns document updates after handover, and how often?
5. When does the group want SSO instead of a staff password?
6. Is a guest-facing pilot wanted this year, and at which property?
